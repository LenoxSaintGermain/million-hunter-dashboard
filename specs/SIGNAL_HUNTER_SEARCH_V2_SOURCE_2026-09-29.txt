Signal Hunter: Business Search Module Update Spec (v2)
Sep 29, 2026 · @Lenox Lucian
Summary
This update makes the business search module verify every number against its source, enforce the mandate as hard gates, and add a game theory layer that predicts how each deal can fail after close.
Problem. In September 2026 three agents (Gemini, Muse, Grokbot) ran the same scout prompt. All three produced correct arithmetic on at least one wrong or incomplete input: a multiple computed on the wrong base, margins divided by asking price, values marked "not disclosed" that were on the page, invented licensing strategies, and off-mandate deals padded into the top five. An earlier codebase audit found sourcing modules running on fabricated data. For a product sold as "kill bad deals before you spend," one fabricated number is fatal.
Goals
1. Zero unverified numbers in any output: every figure carries a source URL and a verbatim capture.
2. 100% of computed ratios recomputed by code, never by the model.
3. Hard mandate gates applied before ranking, so no off-mandate deal reaches PURSUE.
4. Every PURSUE deal ships with a game theory read: the players, the failure games, a protected vs. unprotected expected value, and a walk-away condition.
Non-goals (this version)
* New listing sources or scrapers beyond what the Sonar data layer (spec 005) already provides.
* Broker outreach automation. Output stays read-only; no messages sent.
* Full quality-of-earnings analysis from tax returns. That is a later diligence stage.
* UI changes beyond rendering the new output schema.

	Mandate parameters and hard gates
The mandate moves out of the prompt and into a versioned config file; gates run in code before any model ranks or writes.

P0: gates. Each returns pass, fail, cap (limits the best possible verdict), or unknown.
Gate
	Rule
	On failure
	G1 Price
	Ask within price.min to price.max
	FAIL
	G2 Earnings
	SDE at or above sde.min
	WATCHLIST if in the watchlist band, else FAIL
	G3 Margin
	SDE ÷ revenue at or above 15%
	FAIL; revenue truly absent → HOLD
	G4 Financing
	Not SBA-eligible AND no seller financing → FAIL. Modeled DSCR below 1.25 → FAIL
	FAIL
	G5 Management
	Listing requires an owner-operator or rejects passive buyers → FAIL. No evidence of a management layer → cap at WATCHLIST
	FAIL or cap
	G6 Status
	Under contract, sale pending, or delisted
	Excluded from output
	G7 Watchlist band
	Watchlist requires SDE of $400K to $599K plus a named moat
	Not listed
	G8 Return fewer
	Never fill ranked slots with deals that failed G1 to G5
	Rank only what passes
	G9 Determinism
	Verdict = pure function of gate results and red-flag severities, computed in code
	Model may explain a verdict, never set or override it
	G10 Concentration
	Disclosed top three customers above 50% of revenue → FAIL. One customer above 15% → cap at WATCHLIST
	FAIL or cap
	G11 Moat evidence
	Listing text must show recurring or mandated revenue (maintenance contracts, required inspections, certifications, route density). None found → cap at WATCHLIST
	Cap
	G12 Approximate figures
	Any core figure marked approximate (≈, "about", "roughly")
	HOLD until exact figures are obtained
	Verdict precedence: FAIL > HOLD > WATCHLIST > PURSUE. A deal reaches PURSUE only when every gate passes and no red flag is rated critical.
Acceptance criteria
* ☐ Changing a value in mandate.json changes gate outcomes with no prompt edits.
* ☐ Every report prints the mandate_version it ran against.
* ☐ Re-running the same listing snapshot twice yields the identical verdict.
Extraction and verification rules
The model finds the text; code parses it and does the math. No figure reaches output without a verbatim capture behind it.
V1 Source of truth (P0). Numbers come only from a fetched, listing-level page, never from search snippets or category pages. Store per field: URL, fetch timestamp, and the verbatim text span. If the primary site blocks fetch, a mirror listing page is allowed and every figure from it is tagged source: mirror.
V2 Typed extraction (P0). The model returns text spans; a deterministic parser converts them to values. Fields:
Field
	Type
	Notes
	ask, revenue, sde, ebitda
	integer USD
	Parser tolerates typos such as "$180,00"; flags them instead of guessing
	real_estate
	{status, value, included}
	status: owned, leased, home-based, on request
	inventory, ffe
	{value, included}
	

	employees
	{full_time, part_time}
	

	lease
	{expiration, monthly_rent}
	

	financing
	{sba_eligible, seller_financing, terms_text}
	terms_text kept verbatim
	reason_for_selling, established, listing_date
	string / year / date
	

	licensing_text, management_text
	verbatim
	Feeds gates G5 and red flags
	V3 Ratios in code (P0). The model never computes. Code computes and prints each formula with its inputs:
* multiple = ask ÷ sde
* sde_margin = sde ÷ revenue (never ÷ ask)
* inventory_pct = inventory ÷ revenue
* hard_asset_share = (inventory + ffe + real estate if included) ÷ ask; goodwill = ask − hard assets
* debt_service from the standard amortization formula at config rate and term
* implied_rate solved from any seller-stated payment terms (for example, 204 payments of $9,452.61 on $970K implies about 9.25%)
* dscr = sde ÷ annual debt service
V4 Field completeness (P0). After extraction, a second pass scans the page for each empty field's labels. If a label has a value, extraction failed: retry, then fail the run for that listing. Allowed states are value, not_disclosed (label absent), and on_request (label present, value gated).
V5 Cash waterfall (P0). Every PURSUE and WATCHLIST deal shows: SDE → owner replacement (if the owner works in the business) → market rent (if seller-owned real estate is excluded) → debt service → capex reserve → license qualifier fee → investor preferred return → distributable cash, pre-tax. The word "net" is banned. Defaults live in config and are labeled as assumptions.
V6 No invention (P0). Licensing, legal, and tax claims must cite a source or be written as a verify: item. Customer names appear only if they are on the page. Maintain a banned-terms list, starting with "Qualifying Party Arbitrage."
V7 Cross-listing conflict detector (P1). Fingerprint each listing on category, city or county, ask, FF&E, and year established. When two listings match and SDE or revenue differs by more than 10%, raise financial_year_conflict and generate the broker question: "Which fiscal year does each figure reflect?"

Game theory analysis logic
For every deal that clears the gates, the module identifies who can hurt the buyer after close, which strategic game each creates, the countermove, and how much the countermoves are worth in expected cash. Probabilities and shifts come from a config table, never from the model.
flowchart LR
 A[Verified fields + red flags] --> B[1. Players]
 B --> C[2. Game detectors]
 C --> D[3. Scenario simulation]
 D --> E[4. Moves + walk-away]
 E --> F[Deal report]
Step 1: Players (P0)
Build a player record for each party found in the listing text, plus defaults (seller, broker, lender, competing buyers). Fields: role, source_span, leverage_pre_close and leverage_post_close (0 to 3), and outside_option.
Seller outside option is computed, not guessed: seller_hourly = sde ÷ (owner_hours_per_week × 52). Above $1,000 an hour means a strong fallback: the seller loses little by not selling.
Step 2: Game detectors (P0)
Game
	Detects
	Trigger
	Countermove emitted
	Hold-up
	A party gains leverage after close
	Any player with post-close leverage above pre-close; terms "to be agreed" after closing (R1); short lease (R4)
	Fix price and term in the LOI as a closing condition; add vesting profit share
	Lemons
	Seller knows something the buyer does not
	Multiple below the category's 25th percentile, R9, or R10
	Screening menu: Option A at full ask with a 10% to 15% seller note; Option B at 92% of ask with less seller financing. The seller's choice is the signal
	Defection
	A key person can compete after close
	A player holds a license, customer relationships, or the lead engine (R1, R2)
	Non-compete and non-solicit per named person, not just the seller
	Auction
	Other buyers will bid
	Multiple below category median, or listing under 60 days old
	Compete on certainty: SBA pre-qualification, proof of equity, short diligence window
	Bargaining
	Who can wait longer
	Seller outside option (Step 1)
	Strong fallback: hold price, negotiate terms. Weak fallback (retiring, stale, reduced): negotiate price
	Platform
	One counterparty controls price or access
	R13
	Critical flag; recommend no investment until terms are contractual

P1: Monte Carlo band. 10,000 draws over interest rate (±1.5 points), SDE haircut (0% to 25%), and capex (±30%). Report P10, P50, P90 distributable cash and the probability that DSCR falls below 1.25.
Step 4: Moves and walk-away (P0)
Rank countermoves by expected-value gain. The walk-away condition is the countermove with the largest gain: if the seller refuses it, the verdict drops to WATCHLIST with reason walk_away_triggered.
Worked check (the $1.85M septic deal): R1 and R2 fire. With clean distributable cash of $330K (before investor returns), the tables above give about $234K expected unprotected and $276K protected, and the walk-away is retention agreements for the brother and father.
Acceptance criteria
* ☐ Probabilities, shifts, and haircuts load from config/game_theory.json; the report prints its version.
* ☐ Output is labeled "judgment-based priors, not a statistical forecast."
* ☐ The septic worked check reproduces within ±$5K.
* ☐ A platform-dependent deal (Cybercab fixture) returns FAIL via R13.
