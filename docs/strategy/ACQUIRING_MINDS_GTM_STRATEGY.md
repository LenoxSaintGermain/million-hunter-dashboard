# Acquiring Minds: document-first deal diligence strategy

Date: 2026-09-30. Status: internal strategy proposal; not approved for distribution.

## Recommendation

Position Signal Hunter OS as **deal diligence and decision support: bring your own deal evidence, pressure-test it before committing capital**. Search is one entrypoint, alongside broker introductions, proprietary opportunities, and deals already under review; it is not the product identity.

The best initial Acquiring Minds hypothesis is an educational, evidence-led case workshop for buyers who already have a target. Demonstrate how a promising deal becomes a defensible **pursue, renegotiate, or pass** decision, with unresolved questions intact. Do not start with a sponsorship purchase, a listings pitch, or an AI capability tour.

This is a market-fit hypothesis, not proof of demand. No Acquiring Minds endorsement, access, partnership, customer relationship, audience conversion, or paid inventory has been established. No contact, publication, booking, or spend is authorized by this document.

## Message architecture and differentiation hypotheses

Proposed headline: **Bring the deal. Pressure-test the evidence before committing capital.**

Proposed explanation: “Signal Hunter OS is being developed around the evidence behind an acquisition: what supports the seller's story, what could break it, and what you need to resolve before moving forward. Discovery can start the process; diligence drives the decision.”

Near-term demo CTA: **Walk through an illustrative deal decision.** Do not use “Upload your CIM now” until the ingestion workflow is verified and released.

- Against a sourcing-first frame: lead with a deal already in hand and end with a justified next action, not a longer lead list. S2 makes this relevant; it does not prove competitive superiority.
- Against a generic document summary: the proposed value is contradiction, downside, missing evidence, and consequences—not condensed prose. Demonstrate traceability before claiming it as a working feature.
- Alongside a spreadsheet: make the disputed input and decision condition explicit; retain formulas and human review. Do not claim a spreadsheet cannot do this.
- Alongside professional diligence: organize early questions and escalate them to QoE, legal, tax, operational, and lending specialists. Never advertise “AI QoE,” guaranteed safety, avoided losses, guaranteed financing, or automatic investment approval.
- For an experienced buyer: “What would make you change your mind?” is stronger than “Our agents agree.” Consensus is not verification; dissent and source limits belong in the output.

These are positioning choices to test, not a researched feature comparison against named competitors. Avoid invented testimonials, logos, proprietary access, savings percentages, or claims that most acquisitions fail.

## Concrete demo narrative: “The attractive deal that needs better evidence”

Proposed eight-minute storyboard, not a new shipped demo. Persistent label: **Illustrative — composite deal, not a real customer. Preloaded evidence; no live PDF ingestion or live AI calls.**

Use GT-001 only, which the routed `HunterWalkthrough.tsx` also identifies as its composite source; do not mix it with the different Sunbelt fixture in legacy `Walkthrough.tsx`. Proposed exhibits below must be created and reviewed as synthetic teaching materials before use. No fabricated real contracts, lender letters, customer logos, or guest affiliations.

1. **0:00–1:00 — The decision.** A buyer brings Apex Commercial Cleaning rather than searching for it. Fixture asking price: $2.1m; stated SDE: $720k. Ask whether to pursue, renegotiate, or pass. Display seller-reported status, not “verified earnings.”
2. **1:00–2:30 — Evidence table.** Preload synthetic broker summary, add-back schedule, customer schedule, and owner-role notes mapped to fixture fields. Separate supplied claims, calculated results, assumptions, and missing documents. Explain that a person prepared this packet; nothing is being parsed live.
3. **2:30–4:00 — Pressure test.** Show the fixture's $223k disputed add-backs. If all are excluded, modeled SDE is $497k: $720k minus $223k. Asking-price multiple moves from about 2.92x to 4.23x. This is a sensitivity case, not a professional normalization conclusion; individual adjustments require review. No debt capacity or DSCR claim without complete assumptions.
4. **4:00–5:30 — The counter-case.** Fixture top-two customers represent approximately 54% of revenue. Owner involvement and contract durability matter. The fixture describes a rebid and a verbal arrangement, but the storyboard must not imply the system extracted these from signed contracts. Mark underlying contract verification outstanding; ask what evidence could rebut the concern.
5. **5:30–7:00 — Decision conditions.** Proposed outcome: hold pending evidence, not automatic rejection or approval. Request support for each add-back, customer-level revenue, signed agreements/renewal terms, transition responsibilities, and replacement management cost. Route accounting and contract questions to the relevant professionals. A revised price alone does not resolve customer-transfer risk.
6. **7:00–8:00 — Buyer chooses.** Show a pre-authored decision note: rationale, unresolved items, responsible reviewer, and what would change the decision. Present pursue-with-conditions, renegotiate, and pass as human choices. No LOI sent, financing application, or transaction action.

Acceptance gate before anyone sees it: use frozen fixtures only; block/observe API and provider requests; verify zero calls across the complete route, reload, and every control; verify logged-out access and no dead ends; reconcile all numbers and exhibit provenance; test on desktop/mobile; retain illustrative labels on every shareable frame. Run locally without production credentials. If any gate fails, do not present the interactive version; prepare a separately approved static storyboard. This task does not build or validate that demo.

## Bounded 30/60/90-day plan

All numbers below are proposed experiment caps and decision thresholds, not forecasts, benchmarks, historical results, or current authorization. Day 1 begins only after an operator approves the phase. This task completes the strategy document only. No automatic scheduling, outreach, spend, or publication.

### Days 1–30: prove clarity internally

Proposed owner: product lead with analyst and QA support. Cap: 20 staff hours, $0 media spend, one composite case, one message comparison.

- Prepare the labeled packet/storyboard, a claim ledger, and a question guide. Compare document-first wording with the current discovery-led framing without changing features or implying live upload.
- Ask up to five separately approved internal reviewers to identify what is supported, what is missing, and who decides. Do not count internal reviewers as buyers or evidence of market demand.
- Gate: at least four of five correctly identify all three distinctions, no material arithmetic errors, and no critical claim/roadmap confusion. Record raw counts and objections. Failure means revise the story, not buy traffic.

### Days 31–60: test usefulness with qualified buyers, only if approved

Proposed owner: customer research lead; product lead owns remediation. Cap: 25 additional staff hours, six consented buyer sessions, $0 paid media. Recruitment route and any contact require explicit authorization; no Acquiring Minds access assumed.

- Use only the reviewed composite. Record acquisition stage and volunteered source attribution. Have participants inspect the same packet unassisted, then use the proposed workflow; note order/learning bias rather than claiming causal time savings.
- Ask what next decision changed, which evidence mattered, what was missing, and whether they would return with a permitted packet. Do not collect confidential documents without a separately approved process.
- Gate: at least four of six produce a specific, evidence-linked next action; at least three request a follow-up evaluation; zero unresolved critical false-verification or confidentiality incidents. Small, selected samples are directional. If unmet, revise or pause.

### Days 61–90: test one distribution path, only if approved

Proposed owner: GTM lead with operator approval. Cap: 20 additional staff hours; choose one educational pilot, not multiple channels. Media spend remains $0 unless a separate written budget and terms are approved.

- If earlier gates pass, present an unsent workshop proposal for operator review; only after authorization may an editorial-fit inquiry occur. Partner acceptance, schedule, brand usage, and attribution all require agreement.
- If no partner access is obtained, do not report partnership progress as distribution. Use an independently approved cohort or stop at a ready-to-present asset.
- For a pilot, seek at least ten consented, qualified demo starts within a 30-day measurement window and at least three explicit follow-up requests. Below ten starts, report insufficient sample rather than channel failure. At ten or more starts with fewer than three requests, revise before expanding. These are chosen thresholds, not predicted rates.
- End with go / revise / stop and the evidence for it. No paid scale until usefulness, product-readiness, privacy, and channel economics gates all pass. Ninety days does not create a release deadline for PDF ingestion.

## Measurement contract

Baseline for every market metric: **not_observed**. Keep internal reviewers, qualified buyers, and channel participants separate. Record counts beside rates, source/date, sample definition, and missingness. Never report missing as zero.

| Metric | Definition / interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Qualified starts | Unique consented participants with a specific acquisition decision who begin the demo; exclude staff, duplicates, and untargeted views. |
| Decision completion | Participants documenting an evidence-linked next action / qualified starts. A reasoned pass counts as completion; purchase intent is separate. |
| Claim comprehension | Participants correctly distinguishing seller claim, modeled result, and unknown / participants assessed. Report critical confusion separately. |
| Useful next action | A participant identifies a specific evidence request or conditional decision tied to a case fact; record supporting explanation, not just a satisfaction rating. |
| Follow-up intent / behavior | Explicit follow-up requests / qualified starts; separately count completed follow-ups. Neither is paid conversion. |
| Repeat use | Participants completing a second approved evaluation / eligible participants with another decision in the window. No new deal means ineligible, not automatic churn. |
| Traceability / safety | Material demo claims mapped to fixture or declared assumption / all audited material claims; critical false-verification incidents and confidentiality incidents must be zero before expansion. |
| Effort and economics | Staff hours and any later authorized media cost / qualified completions. Report costs separately; without paying customers, CAC, revenue conversion, and payback are not_observed. |
| Channel attribution | Volunteered discovery source plus an approved tagged link if later deployed; distinguish Acquiring Minds, independent cohort, owned channel, and unknown. Do not attribute all participants to the podcast. |

Do not optimize for deals rejected, time spent in the app, downloads, or webinar registrations alone. Rejection can be wrong; apparent speed can hide missed risks. Capture disconfirming evidence and reasons to continue as well as red flags. Participant notes and aggregate metrics need a consented retention policy; no real deal details in public analytics.
